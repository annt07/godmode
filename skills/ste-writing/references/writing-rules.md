# STE writing rules: summary and sources

This file gives a summary of the public description of ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English. It does not copy the standard or its dictionary. For the full standard, request the free PDF from the official downloads page.

The research note `docs/research/asd-ste100-official-2026-10-08.md` in the godmode repository has the sources and the date of each finding.

## The standard

- **Owner.** ASD (the AeroSpace and Defence Industries Association of Europe) owns ASD-STE100. "ASD-STE100" is a trademark of ASD (EU trade mark 017966390). Tool vendors cannot use the ASD logo, copyright or trademark.
- **Issue.** Issue 9 is the current issue. ASD released it on 15 January 2025. ASD plans Issue 10 for January 2028.
- **Status.** Issue 9 calls STE a "standard", not a "specification".
- **Contents.** The standard has 53 writing rules in 9 sections. It also has a dictionary of about 900 approved words and about 1,200 words not to use.
- **Price.** The standard is free for everyone. ASD gives it as a PDF only.

## Rules that the official site describes

- **One word, one meaning.** STE uses one synonym for each meaning and leaves out the others. Spelling and meanings follow American English (Merriam-Webster).
- **One part of speech for each word.** For example, STE approves "check" as a noun only ("do a check").
- **-ing forms.** STE usually does not permit -ing forms. STE permits an -ing word only as a technical noun or as part of a technical noun.
- **Conditions first.** If the reader must know a condition before a step, put the condition at the start of the sentence.
- **Voice.** Procedures use the imperative. They are not narrative, and they do not use passive voice. Descriptive text also uses active voice. STE permits passive voice only when the actor is unknown.
- **Procedural and descriptive text.** These are two text types. Some rules are different for each type.
- **Technical nouns and technical verbs.** Issue 9 uses these terms, which follow ISO 1087-1:2019. STE permits a word that is not approved only as part of such a term. The term must come from official documents, engineering drawings, company glossaries or terminology databases. Use these terms as little as possible.
- **Principles for other writing.** Short sentences, one topic in each sentence, and active voice.

## Rules from secondary sources only

The official public pages do not give these rules. Wikipedia and other secondary sources give them. The skill uses them, but it does not show their rule numbers as official.

- A sentence has 20 words or fewer in a procedure and 25 words or fewer in a description.
- One instruction in each sentence.
- One topic in each paragraph, with 6 sentences or fewer.
- Keep the subject, the verb and the article.
- Use vertical lists for complex text.
- Start a safety instruction with a command or a condition.
- Simple tenses only.

No public source gives these rules: the 3-word limit for noun clusters, the semicolon ban, the phrasal-verb rule and the verb-not-noun rule. The skill uses them because they make text clearer.

## AI and tools

The STEMG White Paper "ASD-STE100 and AI" (June 2026) says these things:

- Text from an AI can look like STE when it does not obey the rules. Text that looks correct is not verified compliance.
- AI helps authors. It does not replace them. The human author is responsible for the text.
- Mark content that AI helped to write.
- The standard has priority over any tool. STEMG endorses no tool.

The official tools page says that no tool can replace the standard and that a tool cannot change text into STE.
